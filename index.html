Two-minute pupil test

Thanks for helping! This tests whether our software can measure your pupils with an ordinary webcam. Your pupils shrink when the screen gets brighter — it happens on its own, you cannot get it wrong — and the software has to see that happen.

What will happen — about 3 minutes

  • The page goes full screen and asks to use your camera.
  • A grey screen for 15 seconds while it calibrates.
  • Then it alternates dark and bright screens, 10 seconds each, four times.
  • Then the screen stays grey while you rest half a minute, and finally count backwards in your head for 45 seconds. That part tests the pupil without any light change at all.
  • Look at the cross in the middle, blink normally. If the bright screens make you squint, squint — that is fine and useful.

Before you press Begin

  • Sit about an arm's length from the screen, whole face visible.
  • Keep the room lighting steady — the screen should be the only thing changing.
  • Glasses on if you wear them. Everything as normal.

Privacy: the camera turns on when you press Begin and is released the moment the test ends. No video is recorded and nothing is uploaded anywhere — the page computes numbers on your machine, and at the end you download a small results file and send it back yourself. So we can diagnose problems from afar, that file also notes basic facts about your setup: browser, screen size, and your camera's name and picture size — the kind of thing any website can see.
